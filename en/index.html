About

About me

The hard part of industrial inspection is rarely the model. Defect samples are scarce, classes are long-tailed, and the contrast between a defect and normal texture can be a few grey levels. Every time the incoming batch changes, tuned thresholds stop working. I have hit this on both wafer and battery electrode lines.

My approach is to split the problem: make imaging lift the defect out of the texture first, use small-sample strategies to work around scarce labels second, then quantise and deploy to meet line cycle time last. If any of the three fails the metrics will not move, so I do not optimise a single number in isolation.

Inline wafer defect inspection: pushing false calls below 1%

I split detection into candidate recall and fine classification: unsupervised anomaly detection for recall so nothing is missed, few-shot fine-tuning to suppress false calls, then quantised deployment inside the line cycle.
